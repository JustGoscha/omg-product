# Interview question bank

Ask one question at a time. Lead with your best guess so the user can confirm
instead of composing an answer. Skip questions your research already answered;
say what you found instead ("Linear has 14 requests about this, mostly from
maintenance companies — does that match?").

## 1. Trigger

- What happened that made this come up now? (a customer, a churn, an incident, a sales deal)
- Who asked for it, in their words?

## 2. Today

- Walk me through the last time this happened, step by step.
- Which tools are involved today (our app, spreadsheets, mail, phone)?
- Where does it break or take the longest?
- What workaround do people use?

## 3. Who

- Who has this problem? Role, company type, how many accounts.
- Who does not have it, even though they use the same feature?
- Who else is affected downstream (technicians, end customers, finance)?
- Who decides whether we do this? Who must be asked?

## 4. Size of the pain

- How often does it happen (per day, week, quarter)?
- What does it cost: minutes, money, errors, missed visits, churn risk?
- Is there data for this (events, tickets, support time)? If not, what estimate do you trust?

## 5. Success

- If this were solved, what would you see change? Which number?
- What is the number today (even a guess)? What would be good enough?
- By when does it need to be better, and why then?

## 6. Boundaries

- What will people assume is included that we should leave out?
- What must not change (existing workflows, integrations, contracts)?
- Is there a deadline, a budget or a customer commitment?

## 7. Earlier attempts and ideas

- Has anyone tried to solve this before? What happened?
- Which solution ideas have you heard? (park them, don't evaluate yet)

## Stop when

You can write Problem, For whom and Metric without placeholders, and you can
list the open decisions. Usually 6–12 questions. Then summarize back in 5 lines
and ask: "Is this the problem?"
